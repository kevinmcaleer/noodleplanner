How to Use NoodlePlanner on a Phone
===================================

On a screen narrower than 768 pixels, NoodlePlanner switches to a phone
layout. The ribbon and the status bar give way to an app bar, a row of view
chips and a menu, so almost all of the screen shows your plan. Every
control is at least 44 pixels across, so it is big enough to tap.

Tablets keep the full layout, with a touch-sized ribbon (see
:doc:`use-touch-controls`).

Getting Your Plan onto Your Phone
---------------------------------

Your plans live in the browser you made them in. A phone's browser starts
with none of them, so there are two ways to reach a plan from your phone.

**Join a planning session.** On your computer, open the plan and start a
:doc:`planning session <run-a-planning-session>`. The session panel shows a
QR code. Point your phone's camera at it and open the link: the join page
opens with the code already filled in. Enter your name and tap **Join
session**. Everything you change on the phone reaches the plan on your
computer.

.. figure:: /_static/img/how-to/ph-08-session-qr.png
   :alt: The planning session panel, with the six-digit code, the join page's address and a QR code under them
   :width: 60%

   The session panel. The QR code opens the join page with the code
   already filled in.

On a phone, the join page shows the plan as cards, the same as **Tasks**
below. Tap a row to change its duration, % complete or comment, or tick it
off. **Cards** and **Canvas** at the top switch to the whiteboard itself,
which works better on a larger screen.

.. figure:: /_static/img/how-to/ph-09-joiner-cards.png
   :alt: A phone that has joined a planning session, showing the plan as cards, one open with its tasks listed
   :width: 40%

   A phone in a planning session.

**Open the plan file.** Save the plan to a file you can reach from your
phone, such as one in iCloud Drive or Google Drive, and open it with
**File** ▸ **Open** on the phone. The plan is then kept in the phone's
browser. Changes you make on the phone come back as a downloaded file, so
you copy them back yourself.

Finding Your Way Around
-----------------------

.. figure:: /_static/img/how-to/ph-01-dashboard.png
   :alt: The dashboard on a phone: the app bar with the plan's name, the view chips under it, and the dashboard in one column
   :width: 40%

   The dashboard on a phone.

**The app bar** runs across the top:

- **☰** opens the menu.
- The plan's name and its RAG dot. Tap the name to switch to another plan.
- **Search** finds tasks, and **the bell** shows the plan's messages.
- **⋯** opens every command for the current view, the same ones the ribbon
  has, each with a line explaining what it does. **Undo** and **Redo** are
  at the top of the list.

**The view chips** under it are one tap away from the views made for a
phone: Dashboard, Tasks, Outline, Board, Calendar and RAID Log. Swipe the
row sideways to see them all.

**The menu** (☰) lists every view, grouped as on the ribbon. The views that
work well on a phone come first in each group. Views that need a larger
screen come last and say so. The menu also has the **Project**,
**Programme** and **Portfolio** switch, and the **Markdown** editor, which
has a full screen of its own on a phone.

.. figure:: /_static/img/how-to/ph-02-drawer.png
   :alt: The menu open over the dashboard: the scope switch, then the Plan views, with Gantt and the others below marked Best on a larger screen
   :width: 40%

   The menu. Views that need a larger screen are listed last and marked.

**The + button** in the corner adds whatever the view holds: a task in
Tasks or Outline, a card on the Board, an item in the RAID log.

Working with Tasks
------------------

On a phone, **Tasks** shows your plan as cards, one for each top-level
summary task. Each card shows the summary's progress, dates, RAG status and
the people on it. Tap a card's arrow to open it and see its tasks. The app
remembers which cards you have open, and the Gantt chart's own collapse
buttons follow the same choice.

.. figure:: /_static/img/how-to/ph-03-plan-list.png
   :alt: Tasks as cards. The Design card is open, listing its three tasks with tick boxes, dates and people; the add-a-task field is at the foot of the screen
   :width: 40%

   Tasks as cards, with the add-a-task field at the foot of the screen.

- **Tick a task** to mark it 100% complete. Untick it to set it back to 0%.
- **Tap a task's name** to open all its details.
- **Add a task** by typing in the field at the foot of the screen. It goes
  into the card you have open, and you can type the same shorthand as in
  the editor: ``Review copy @sam 2d`` shows *Sam* and *2 days* above the
  field before you add it.
- **Move a task:** tap **⋯** on its row (or swipe the row to the left, or
  press and hold it) for **Indent**, **Outdent**, **Move up**, **Move
  down**, **Duplicate** and **Delete**. To drag a task somewhere else in
  the list, press and hold it, then drag. Swiping a row to the right marks
  it complete.

Details and Forms
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

A task's details, like every other form, fill the screen on a phone. **←**
at the top goes back. Actions you need less often, such as deleting the
task, are under **⋯**. Fields are large enough to type in without the page
zooming.

   :alt: A task's details filling the phone's screen, with a back arrow, the task's name and a more-actions button at the top and Done at the foot
   :width: 40%

   A task's details on a phone.

Logs and Tables
---------------

The RAID log, Actions, Milestones, Stakeholders, Budget and the other
tables show one card per row on a phone. A card shows the row's title,
with its status and one or two other key columns under it. Tap **⌄** to see
every other column, including the row's own buttons. In the RAID log,
Actions, Milestones and the dashboard, tapping the card itself opens the
item.

.. figure:: /_static/img/how-to/ph-05-raid-cards.png
   :alt: The RAID log as cards. The first card is open, showing the item's description, owner, mitigation and the rest under its title and status
   :width: 40%

   The RAID log, with one card opened to show all its columns.

The dashboard is one column on a phone, in the order you check it: the
plan's status, the next milestones, what is up next, and the open risks.

The Calendar
------------

On a phone, the calendar opens on an agenda. It lists what is on today,
then what starts or is due on each of the next seven days. Tap an item to
open its task. **Month** switches to the month grid, and the calendar
remembers which one you chose.

.. figure:: /_static/img/how-to/ph-06-calendar-agenda.png
   :alt: The calendar as an agenda: today, then the days ahead, each listing the tasks that start or are due
   :width: 40%

   The calendar's agenda.

Views Best on a Larger Screen
-----------------------------

The Gantt chart, the timeline, the mind map, the whiteboard's canvas and a
few other views are wide by nature. You can still open them on a phone,
and a note at the foot of the screen suggests the nearest view that works
well on a phone: **Open Tasks** from the Gantt chart, **Open Milestones**
from the timeline, **Show as cards** from the whiteboard. Close the note
with **×** and it stays closed for that view.

.. figure:: /_static/img/how-to/ph-07-larger-screen-notice.png
   :alt: The Gantt chart on a phone, with a note at the foot reading Gantt is best on a larger screen, and an Open Tasks button
   :width: 40%

   The Gantt chart, with its note pointing at Tasks.

Choosing the Layout Yourself
----------------------------

NoodlePlanner picks the layout from the width of the screen: a phone under
768 pixels, a tablet up to 1024, and a desktop above that. To choose one
yourself, open **Settings** ▸ **Layout** and pick **Phone**, **Tablet** or
**Desktop**. For example, you might want the desktop layout on a tablet
with a keyboard. **Auto** goes back to choosing by screen width. The choice
is kept in this browser only.
